ATS Resume Advice

ATS Resume Tips for Canadian Job Seekers

Practical resume formatting, keyword, and recruiter-readability guidance for job seekers across Canada.

Many job seekers worry about ATS.

ATS stands for Applicant Tracking System. Employers use these systems to collect, sort, and review job applications.

An ATS does not hire people. Employers hire people.

But your resume still needs to be easy for the system and the recruiter to read.

A good ATS-friendly resume is clear, targeted, and simple. It uses the right keywords without sounding robotic.

What Is an ATS?

An Applicant Tracking System helps employers manage applications.

When you apply online, your resume may go into an ATS before a recruiter sees it.

The system may read your resume, store your information, and help recruiters search for candidates by job title, skill, location, education, or experience.

This means your resume needs to be readable and searchable.

ATS-Friendly Does Not Mean Plain and Weak

Some people think an ATS resume must look boring.

That is not true.

An ATS-friendly resume can still look professional.

The key is to avoid formatting that blocks the system from reading your information.

Focus on:

  • Clear headings
  • Standard sections
  • Simple layout
  • Relevant keywords
  • Strong bullet points
  • Easy-to-read content

Use Standard Resume Headings

ATS systems and recruiters both understand common headings.

Use headings such as:

  • Professional Summary
  • Skills
  • Work Experience
  • Education
  • Certifications
  • Volunteer Experience
  • Projects

Avoid creative headings like:

  • My Journey
  • Career Story
  • What I Bring
  • Where I Have Been

Creative headings may look interesting, but they can reduce clarity.

Choose a Simple Resume Format

Use a clean format.

Avoid:

  • Text boxes
  • Tables
  • Icons
  • Photos
  • Charts
  • Columns that may not read properly
  • Heavy graphics
  • Unusual fonts
  • Headers and footers with important contact details

Use a clear font and simple spacing.

Good resume formats are easy to scan. If you are choosing between visual templates, this related guide explains why simple resume formatting often performs better in Canada.

Put Contact Information in the Main Body

Your name, phone number, email, city, province, and LinkedIn URL should be easy to find.

Do not place important contact details only in the header or footer. Some systems may not read them properly.

Example:

Julia Cher
Toronto, ON
info@hiremenowresumes.ca
LinkedIn: linkedin.com/in/yourname

Match the Job Posting

ATS strategy starts with the job posting.

Review the posting carefully.

Look for repeated words related to:

  • Job title
  • Required skills
  • Software
  • Certifications
  • Industry terms
  • Tasks
  • Education
  • Years of experience

Then include the relevant ones naturally in your resume.

Do not copy the entire job posting. Use the language that matches your real experience.

Use the Right Job Title

If your past title was unusual, you can clarify it.

Example:

Client Experience Specialist
Equivalent to Customer Service Representative

Or:

Office Coordinator
Administrative Assistant / Office Support

This helps recruiters understand how your experience connects to the role.

Do not invent job titles. Clarify them honestly.

Build a Strong Skills Section

A skills section helps recruiters and systems quickly understand your fit.

Example for administrative roles:

Administrative Support | Customer Service | Data Entry | Scheduling | Microsoft Office | File Management | Email Communication | Records Management | Time Management | Team Coordination

Example for project roles:

Project Coordination | Reporting | Stakeholder Communication | Meeting Support | Process Improvement | Risk Tracking | Documentation | Microsoft Excel | Team Collaboration

Use skills that match your target jobs. A targeted resume writing service can help align your summary, skills, and work experience with the roles you want.

Write Bullet Points With Keywords and Results

Weak bullet point:

Responsible for customer service.

Better bullet point:

Responded to customer inquiries by phone and email, resolved service issues, updated client records, and supported daily office operations.

Stronger bullet point:

Handled high-volume customer inquiries by phone and email while updating client records, resolving service issues, and maintaining accurate documentation.

Good bullet points include action, skill, and context.

Do Not Keyword Stuff

Keyword stuffing means adding the same words too many times.

Example:

Customer service, customer service, customer service, customer service.

This looks unnatural.

Use keywords in a normal way across your summary, skills, and work experience.

Your resume should be written for both the system and the human reader.

Save the File Correctly

Use a simple file name.

Example:

FirstName_LastName_Resume.pdf

Or:

FirstName_LastName_Administrative_Assistant_Resume.pdf

Many employers accept PDF. Some systems ask for Word documents.

Follow the instructions in the job posting.

Avoid Resume Design Mistakes

Do not use a resume template that looks beautiful but is hard to read.

Common problems include:

  • Two-column layouts
  • Tiny font
  • Icons instead of words
  • Skills shown only as graphics
  • Contact details hidden in the header
  • Important information inside text boxes
  • Decorative sections that distract from content

A resume should look professional, but the content matters most.

Should You Use an Indeed Resume and a Word Resume?

It can be useful to have both.

An Indeed resume can help with search visibility on Indeed.

A Word or PDF resume gives you more control over formatting, structure, and strategy.

Use your professionally written resume when applying directly to roles, sending applications by email, or uploading to employer websites.

Quick Recruiter Reality

ATS matters, but it is not the whole story.

A resume can pass basic system scanning and still fail if the content is weak.

Recruiters look for relevance, clarity, results, and fit.

The best resume is both ATS-friendly and recruiter-friendly.

FAQ

What does ATS mean?

ATS means Applicant Tracking System. Employers use it to manage job applications.

Do Canadian employers use ATS?

Many medium and large employers in Canada use online application systems. Smaller employers may review resumes manually.

Is PDF or Word better for ATS?

Follow the job posting instructions. If no format is listed, PDF is often acceptable, but Word may be safer for some older systems.

Should I use columns on my resume?

A simple one-column format is usually safer.

Should I add keywords to my resume?

Yes, but only when they match your real experience. Use keywords naturally.

Can an ATS reject my resume automatically?

Some systems help filter candidates, but recruiters and hiring teams still make hiring decisions. Your resume should be clear for both technology and people.

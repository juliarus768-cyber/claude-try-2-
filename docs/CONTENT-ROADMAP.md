# Three-month editorial roadmap

Planning window: November 2026–January 2027. Six proposed articles, two per month. These are briefs, not published articles or scheduled automations. All examples must be explicitly illustrative unless Julia supplies permission and verified client material. Check current inventory again before drafting.

Existing English coverage: ATS formatting, resume design/photos, employment gaps, cover letters, LinkedIn and interview STAR responses. Existing Russian coverage: resume structure, ATS, gaps and Canadian resume versus European CV. Newcomer landing pages cover the service journey. The articles below should add worked examples and decision tools, not repeat those pages.

## November 2026 — make international experience legible

### 1. How to Adapt International Work Experience for a Canadian Resume

- Intent: practical, informational guidance for applicants adapting overseas roles.
- Keyword theme: international work experience on Canadian resume; adapt overseas experience.
- Proposed URL: `/international-work-experience-canadian-resume.html`.
- Reader problem: relevant experience is buried in country-specific terminology and duties.
- Examples: an explicitly fictional order-coordination role, a short employer descriptor, and a posting-to-evidence worksheet. Show what stays unchanged: employer, country, actual scope and dates. Explain what evidence is missing rather than inventing numbers.
- CTA: compare Resume Strategy for one agreed target; use Career Clarity if the target is unclear.
- Links: newcomer landing page, resume-writing services, `services.html#resume-strategy`, ATS article, employment-gap article. Add a contextual link back from the newcomer page after publication.
- Distinct value: step-by-step worked adaptation with a worksheet; the landing page introduces the principles and service.

### 2. How to Explain Foreign Job Titles to Canadian Employers

- Intent: resolve uncertainty about translation and equivalency.
- Keyword theme: foreign job title Canadian resume; translate job title accurately.
- Proposed URL: `/explain-foreign-job-titles-canadian-resume.html`.
- Reader problem: a literal title hides the actual function or suggests the wrong level.
- Examples: fictional administrative and operations titles with literal translation, accurate functional explanation in parentheses and a misleading seniority upgrade to avoid. Add a checklist for verifying title, duties and regulated designations.
- CTA: explore targeted resume support; invite a free fit call if scope is unclear.
- Links: international-experience article, newcomer page, resume-writing services and `services.html#resume-strategy`.
- Distinct value: title-level accuracy and evidence, rather than a general CV-format comparison. Russian adaptation should link to the existing CV comparison instead of recreating it.

## December 2026 — diagnose the application problem

### 3. Why Your Resume Is Not Getting Interviews in Canada: A Practical Review

- Intent: troubleshoot low response without assuming the resume is the only cause.
- Keyword theme: resume not getting interviews Canada; job applications no response.
- Proposed URL: `/resume-not-getting-interviews-canada.html`.
- Reader problem: unsure whether to change the resume, target roles or application approach.
- Examples: a fictional application log showing target mismatch versus unclear evidence; a simple review sequence covering eligibility, role fit, document clarity and application instructions. Do not invent market response-rate benchmarks.
- CTA: Free Resume Diagnostic first; Career Clarity when direction remains unclear; Resume Strategy only when the target and document need are clear.
- Links: ATS article for formatting details, newcomer page, diagnostic, `services.html#career-clarity` and resume-writing services.
- Distinct value: pre-interview troubleshooting. Existing STAR article addresses a different stage: no callback after an interview. Do not duplicate its advice or its URL.

### 4. Resume Writer or Career Coach: Which Service Do You Need?

- Intent: commercial investigation and informed service selection.
- Keyword theme: resume writer vs career coach Canada; resume help or career direction.
- Proposed URL: `/resume-writer-or-career-coach.html`.
- Reader problem: considering paid help without knowing what type of support fits.
- Examples: fictional scenarios for a clear target with weak documents, unclear direction, and two distinct directions. Include a “self-help may be enough” case and a scope comparison that matches current package terms.
- CTA: service-specific anchors based on the scenario; one optional free fit call for unresolved questions.
- Links: services comparison, `services.html#career-clarity`, `#resume-strategy`, `#multi-path`, diagnostic and existing resume-writing page.
- Distinct value: decision guide, rather than repeating the package catalogue. Recheck prices and inclusions at drafting time.

## January 2027 — demonstrate the editing process

### 5. Canadian Resume Before and After: Explaining International Experience

- Intent: learn from a concrete, annotated example.
- Keyword theme: Canadian resume before and after; international experience resume example.
- Proposed URL: `/canadian-resume-before-after-international-experience.html`.
- Reader problem: principles make sense but applying them to a whole experience section is difficult.
- Examples: one clearly fictional resume excerpt before/after, with annotations for employer context, title explanation, transferable skills and verified evidence. Include an editor's question where the fictional source lacks detail; do not fill gaps with made-up achievements. Provide accessible text, not image-only examples.
- CTA: compare Resume Strategy for one target; link to Multi-Path only when discussing two genuinely different directions.
- Links: November experience and title articles, newcomer page, ATS guide and resume-writing services.
- Distinct value: full annotated excerpt. Avoid repeating November's single-bullet example or implying that a formatting change produced interviews.

### 6. What Canadian Employers Mean by “Canadian Experience”

- Intent: understand an ambiguous requirement and decide how to respond honestly.
- Keyword theme: Canadian experience job requirements; international experience Canada.
- Proposed URL: `/what-employers-mean-canadian-experience.html`.
- Reader problem: unsure which actual job requirements can be demonstrated through previous experience.
- Examples: fictional postings that ask about local customers, software, standards or communication; a matrix separating demonstrable skills from credentials or experience genuinely missing. Show truthful questions to ask an employer.
- CTA: self-assessment and Career Clarity if target selection needs work; newcomer guidance for presenting existing evidence.
- Links: newcomer page, international-experience article, `services.html#career-clarity`, resume-writing services and relevant official employment resources selected at drafting time.
- Distinct value: interpret specific requirements rather than generalize about all Canadian employers. Verify any employment-law, licensing or human-rights statement with current official provincial sources before publication; avoid legal conclusions.

## Editorial and measurement process

Have Julia review business claims, examples and service scope before publication. Use one descriptive H1, unique metadata and a canonical URL. Add only relevant internal links and correct sitemap dates when each article is actually published. Russian adaptations should add local language value and avoid duplicating the four existing resources. Use reciprocal hreflang only when a complete equivalent exists.

Measure relevant search queries and landing-page visits in Search Console/consented GA4, then diagnostic clicks, service visits, booking interest, verified inquiries and completed consultations where measurable. Review inquiry relevance with Julia using an agreed definition, without placing inquiry contents in analytics. Establish baselines first; do not claim growth from publication alone.
